What you paid for

The operating system on this drive is free software. You can download exactly the same thing, at no cost, from system76.com/pop. You are not paying for the software and we are not selling it to you.

What you paid for is the drive itself, our time writing it, and the checking: we verify the image against the publisher’s official SHA-256 fingerprint before writing, and we read the drive back afterward to confirm it stored the image correctly. A drive that looks fine but will not boot is the usual way this goes wrong, and that check is what you are buying.

Keep this notice — it is your source-code offer

Print one of these for every drive you sell or give away. Letter paper, single sided. Fill in the build stamp at the top by hand, or from the output of the boot-stick script.

Two blanks you must fill in before printing a batch:

  • The mailing address in the source-code offer. GPLv2 requires that someone be able to request the source on physical media by mail, so a real postal address or PO box is needed. I have left it blank rather than invent one.
  • The build stamp, per unit — release, build number, channel, SHA-256, and date.

Why the $25 figure: the GPL lets you charge no more than your reasonable cost of physically performing the source distribution. $25 covers a drive and postage. Do not raise it — overcharging for source is the classic way to trigger an enforcement complaint.

Sell the Intel/AMD image, not the NVIDIA one. The NVIDIA driver ships under NVIDIA’s own license. Section 1.1(d) grants the right to distribute it alongside a Linux kernel, but section 2.7 separately says you may not sell the software except as expressly granted — and "sell" and "distribute" are listed as different acts. Nobody has resolved that tension publicly. The Intel/AMD image has no such clause and Pop!_OS installs the NVIDIA driver after setup anyway, so defaulting to Intel/AMD removes the only real legal question in this product. If a customer specifically needs the NVIDIA image, you must also hand them a printed copy of the NVIDIA Driver License Agreement — section 1.1(d)(ii) makes your right to distribute it conditional on that.

Behind the counter: for each build you sell, archive the source once (using casper/filesystem.manifest from the ISO as the package list) and keep it three years past your last sale of that build, labeled with the build number and SHA-256. An offer you cannot honor is worse than no offer. Keep a dated sales log so you know when each three-year clock expires.

Worth ten minutes: email trademark@system76.com describing exactly what you plan to do. System76 publishes no Pop!_OS trademark policy, so this either gets you written comfort or an early warning. Their COSMIC policy is permissive and explicitly allows truthful referential use, which is a good sign.
