Setup sheet — do this before the Google profile

Get listed where Google can see you

Google verification goes worse for a business it has no independent record of. These listings are the record. None of them will bring you a customer directly — that is not what they are for. They exist so that when Google asks “is this a real business,” something other than your own website says yes.

00

Decide your address first

Every listing below asks for one, and they all have to match — including the one Google holds privately. Changing it later is the expensive mistake.

Your two real options

OptionWorks for Google?Trade-off
Your home address, marked not-for-display wherever the site allows Yes Simplest and fully consistent. Some directories will still show it, so assume it becomes findable.
A rented mailbox with a street address (a UPS Store box, not a PO Box) Risky Keeps your home private, but Google treats mailbox addresses as virtual offices and suspends listings that use them. Fine for BBB and DataAxle, not for Google.

A plain PO Box is not an option — Google rejects them outright.

What I would do: use your home address everywhere, hidden on Google, and request non-display on BBB and DataAxle. Consistency is the thing being measured, and a mismatch between what Google privately holds and what the directories publish is exactly the discrepancy that gets a listing flagged.

01

The block that goes everywhere

Paste this character for character into every listing. Do not abbreviate, reorder or improvise a variation.

Hahn Technologies Corp [YOUR STREET ADDRESS] New Bern, NC 28560 (252) 626-6236 hahntechnologiescorp@gmail.com https://newberncomputerrepair.com

“Corp” not “Corporation.” “Hahn Technologies Corp” not “Hahn Tech.” Matching is what is being measured. One directory saying “Hahn Tech LLC” undoes the work of three that got it right.

Use 626-6236, never 253-9629. That second number belongs to Faith & Fitness / Koala Tea Kratom, and sharing it across two companies is how Google concludes the two listings are really one business.

02

Data Axle / InfoUSA

The oldest business-data compiler in the US. Banks, mapping services and search engines buy from them, so a record here propagates quietly into a lot of places at once. This is the highest-value item on the page and almost nobody does it.

Find their business-listing update form (search “Data Axle add or update my business listing” — they have moved the page more than once, so I would rather send you to the current one than a stale link). Submitting a listing is free. Decline the upsells; you want the record, not the marketing package.

Allow several weeks to propagate. This is the main reason to do it first.

03

Better Business Bureau

A BBB record is an independent statement that the business exists, and it carries real weight with older customers — who are most of your market.

Know which part costs money

A basic BBB listing is free. BBB Accreditation — the badge and the rating — is a paid annual membership, commonly several hundred dollars for a small business. Their sales team is persistent.

For the purpose on this page, the free listing does the job. Accreditation is a marketing decision to make later, once you know whether your customers actually ask about it.

Search for the BBB serving Eastern North Carolina and use their “add a business” form. If a record already exists under a similar name, claim rather than duplicate.

04

Apple Business Connect

Free, roughly twenty minutes, and it feeds Apple Maps and Siri. Your customers skew older and skew iPhone, and hardly any local competitor has claimed theirs.

At businessconnect.apple.com. Same block as above, same categories as the Google sheet.

05

Bing Places

Free, and it can import most fields from your Google profile in one step — so it is easiest done after Google is verified, not before.

Increasingly feeds AI assistants that answer “who repairs computers near me” without showing a map at all. Cheap insurance.

06

Facebook Page and Nextdoor

Both free, and in a town this size they are discovery channels rather than formalities.

  • Nextdoor is probably your highest-return non-Google channel. Your stated customer — someone helping a parent or grandparent — is exactly who is on it, and New Bern, Trent Woods and Fairfield Harbour are dense with them. Recommendations there travel by word of mouth in a way Google reviews do not.
  • Facebook matters for the local buy/sell/recommend groups. Post in those as a person, not as a business — group admins remove ads, and you will get further answering a question than posting a flyer.
07

Then, and only then, Google

Once Data Axle and BBB have had a couple of weeks to settle, work through the Google Business Profile sheet.

Order matters here. Verification tends to go better when independent records already exist, and you may only get one clean attempt at the video.

—

Do not buy any of this

Skip these

  • “500 directory submissions” packages and citation subscriptions (Yext and similar) — $500–1,000 a year to appear on sites nobody in New Bern will ever visit. For one location in one small market, the handful above done by hand beats all of it.
  • BBB Accreditation, for now. The free listing is what this page needs.
  • Yelp advertising. Claim the free listing, fill it in, then ignore their sales calls. At $79–149 a job the ad math never works.
  • Anything promising to “fix” your citations for a monthly fee. You have six listings. You can maintain six listings.

The links that actually move a local business here are not directories at all: the New Bern Chamber, a church or Rotary mention, a piece in New Bern Now or the Sun Journal, sponsoring a youth team, or a free workshop at the library or senior center. One local news mention outweighs fifty directory entries.
